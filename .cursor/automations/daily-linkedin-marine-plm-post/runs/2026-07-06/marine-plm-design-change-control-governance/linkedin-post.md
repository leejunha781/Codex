# 🚢 In Marine PLM, AI Can Draft the Change Impact — But Someone Still Has to Own the Disposition

Most shipyard rework I have seen does not come from a missed design. It comes from a design change that moved through CAD but never fully reconciled with the BOM, the supplier package, and the class/test records before steel was cut.

AI now drafts engineering-change impact analyses in minutes — cross-referencing affected parts, tag lists, interface points, and downstream ERP/MES orders faster than any manual review. That acceleration is genuine. But an AI-drafted impact summary is a proposal, not a disposition. Until a named change owner confirms scope, configuration baseline, effectivity, and hold criteria, a "low-impact" label is just an unverified opinion moving toward the production line.

The governance shift is concrete: let AI widen the search for impact, but keep the engineering-change board accountable for the decision, the effectivity point, and the traceable record that survives audit and warranty years later. In Marine PLM, the risk is never the speed of analysis — it is an untraced change that quietly desynchronizes the digital thread across CAD, ERP, MES, and document control.

## 🔧 Practical application points

1. **Bind change impact to the configuration baseline** — Correlate each ECR/ECO with its affected BOM revisions, CAD releases, and interface points on one baseline so effectivity and roll-back are reconstructable, not guessed.
2. **Classify disposition with auditable rules, not vibes** — Encode impact thresholds, class/regulatory triggers, and mandatory-hold conditions as repeatable pass/hold/escalate logic, and route every hold to a named board owner.
3. **Emit change evidence packs for yard and support** — Auto-generate structured records (affected items, interface deltas, supplier notifications, class/test re-verification, effectivity cut-in) ready for shipyard adoption gates and post-delivery support reuse.

**💡 Takeaway:** AI can find the ripple effects of a design change faster than any team — but in Marine PLM, accountable disposition, configuration effectivity, and a traceable change record are what keep a fast analysis from becoming expensive rework.
