# 🛰️ LEO Satellite Terminal Commissioning Is an Evidence Chain — Not an Install-and-Hope Event

A LEO terminal can show a green link icon on day one and still fail the first operational storm.

In naval and commercial maritime programs, the real risk is not antenna installation. It is accepting a terminal without a traceable commissioning chain: pointing proof, modem lock stability, RF margin under motion, failover behavior, and customer-facing acceptance records.

## Why programs get surprised after “successful” installation

Teams often treat commissioning as a calendar milestone. Install, ping the network, capture one screenshot, and move on. That works until the vessel sails, the platform pitches, the gateway policy changes, or the backup path never gets exercised.

For LEO maritime terminals, the accountable question is simple: **what evidence proves the system is operationally ready — not just electrically connected?**

## 🔧 Three practical application points

**1. Gate pointing and link stability before FAT sign-off**  
Capture antenna pointing verification, modem lock duration, and link-loss events under representative motion or simulated conditions. A one-minute green status bar is not commissioning evidence.

**2. Treat failover as a tested function, not a configuration checkbox**  
Primary and backup paths need exercised scenarios, threshold logs, and named owners. If failover is only documented, it is not validated.

**3. Package FAT/SAT records for shore review and lifecycle support**  
Engineering, integration, and customer stakeholders need the same evidence set: test scripts, pass/fail thresholds, exception dispositions, and final acceptance signatures. That package becomes the baseline for post-delivery support.

## Practical takeaway ✅

LEO terminal commissioning should read like a systems integration deliverable: measurable gates, accountable owners, and reviewable records. That discipline protects sea acceptance, reduces rework at sail-away, and gives support teams a defensible baseline after handover.
